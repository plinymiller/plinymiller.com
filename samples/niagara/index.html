02 — Parameter reference

Niagara N4 LoopPoint slots

The workbench uses the interacting equation from the kitControl guide. KP multiplies the whole parenthesis, so proportional, integral, and derivative all scale together.

ES = PV − SP
Out = bias ± KP × ( ES + KI × ∫ES dtmin + KD × dES/dtsec )
Direct uses +. Reverse uses −. KI is repeats per minute. KD is seconds.

integralConstant

Repeats per minute. A larger value repeats the proportional correction sooner, so reset gets faster. At 0.5 repeats/min the integral matches the current proportional term about every two minutes. Set it to 0 and the offset of proportional-only control stays. Controllers that take integral time Ti in seconds or minutes are the other way up: there, a bigger number is slower. Ti in minutes is about 1 / integralConstant.

derivativeConstant

Leave it at 0 on HVAC loops. It is derivative time in seconds, and it reacts to the rate of change of the error, scaled by the proportional constant. A noisy flow station or static-pressure transmitter turns that rate into damper or VFD chatter. The workbench shows it: open the VAV preset, set deadband to 0, and raise derivative.

loopAction

Direct means the output rises when the controlled variable rises. Use it when more output brings the variable down: a cooling valve, a chilled-water coil. Reverse means the output rises when the controlled variable falls. Use it when more output brings the variable up: a heating valve, supply-fan static pressure, VAV airflow. If the device runs to the stop as soon as the loop enables, flip the action before you touch a gain.

executeTime

The scan, in seconds. The usual default is 1. Duct static pressure needs that, or 0.5. A 5 or 10 second scan on a fan adds enough delay to overshoot. Zone temperature can run at 10–30 seconds. Integral is accumulated in real minutes, so a faster scan does not turn the reset up. It only sees the plant sooner. Fast loops belong next to the I/O when the points are not on the same JACE.

proportionalConstant

Gain on the error, in percent of output per unit of the controlled variable. A starting value is output span divided by the throttling range you want. On an interacting LoopPoint this gain also scales integral and derivative. At 0, new proportional moves stop and further reset stops accumulating.

integralConstant

Repeats per minute. While the error holds still, the reset adds another copy of the current proportional correction this many times each minute. Higher is faster. Too much, on a coil or a duct with transport lag, and the valve swings slowly past setpoint. Zero leaves the proportional offset in place.

derivativeConstant

Derivative time in seconds. It kicks the output when the error is changing quickly, including the instant a setpoint steps, and it multiplies sensor noise on every scan. HVAC loops leave it at 0. If a loop needs damping, reduce the proportional and integral constants and slow the output with rampTime.

loopAction

Direct or Reverse. Direct: cooling coil, chilled-water valve, any loop where more output lowers the variable. Reverse: heating coil, hot-water valve, duct static on a supply fan, VAV damper airflow. The sign applies to the whole PID term, so a wrong action drives the accumulated reset the wrong way and the output heads for the stop.

rampTime

Seconds for the output to travel from minimumOutput to maximumOutput. At 60 seconds the output can move about 1.7% per second. At 0 the valve or VFD can step. Set it near the real stroke so the loop cannot command a move the actuator has not finished. Some kits also expose maxDelta, the maximum change allowed in one scan. Same idea.

bias

Percent added after the PID calculation. With integral at 0, bias is the output when the error is zero, which is the only way a P-only loop holds a load. With integral running, the reset winds until the plant is satisfied and the bias is absorbed. Leave it at 0 when integralConstant is above 0.

deadband

Engineering units of the controlled variable. While the absolute error is inside the band, the loop treats the error as zero: the output holds and the integral does not wind. Size it to the noise you can see on the trend, about 0.02–0.05 in.wc, 0.3–0.5°F, or 20–40 CFM. Wider than the error you can accept, and the loop parks short of setpoint.

maximumOutput / minimumOutput

Hard clamps, usually 0 and 100. A minimum above 0 keeps a fan from stopping or holds a minimum ventilation position. If out sits on either clamp, the plant is out of capacity or the action is backwards. More integral only winds up against the stop. Fix the plant or the action, then retune.

disableAction

Where out goes when loopEnable is false. Hold keeps the last value, Zero forces 0, Min and Max force the clamps. Link loopEnable to the fan or pump proof. A loop left enabled with the air handler off winds the integral, and the valve slams when the fan starts. Match the disable action to the fail position, and keep rampTime in the path so the restart slews.

executeTime

Seconds between PID calculations. Static pressure and VAV airflow want 0.5–1 s. A supply-air or coil loop is comfortable at 1–2 s. A zone can use 10–30 s. Slowing the scan on a fast plant adds phase lag: the correction arrives after the duct has already moved, and the fan overshoots. The workbench static preset shows that if you set executeTime to 10.

04 — Troubleshooting

What is my loop doing?

Pick the picture on the trend. Change one slot, then watch setpoint, controlled variable, and out together.

Likely causes

  • proportionalConstant is high enough that each correction crosses setpoint.
  • derivativeConstant is above 0 and the sensor is noisy.
  • executeTime is slow on a fast plant, so the fan correction arrives late.
  • deadband is 0 and the noise is passed straight to the output.

LoopPoint adjustments

  • Cut proportionalConstant by about a third and trend another step.
  • Set derivativeConstant to 0.
  • On duct static, set executeTime to 1 second, or 0.5.
  • Set deadband to the noise floor: 0.02–0.05 in.wc, 0.3–0.5°F, or 20–40 CFM.
  • Raise rampTime so a VFD cannot reverse direction on every scan.

Likely causes

  • integralConstant is too high for the lag in the coil, damper, or duct.
  • rampTime is 0, so the loop keeps integrating a move the actuator has not finished.

LoopPoint adjustments

  • Halve integralConstant. On this object a larger number is a faster reset.
  • Leave derivativeConstant at 0. Derivative will not calm a slow swing.
  • Set rampTime near the stroke time of the valve or damper.
  • Trend at least three cycles before you change it again.

Likely causes

  • integralConstant is 0, so proportional-only control keeps an offset.
  • out is sitting on minimumOutput or maximumOutput.
  • deadband is wider than the error you are willing to live with.
  • The valve is not stroking, or the coil cannot carry the load.

LoopPoint adjustments

  • Raise integralConstant one small step: about 0.2–0.4 repeats/min on a temperature loop, a bit higher on static pressure.
  • If out is on the clamp, stop adding integral. Check loopAction, the stroke, and whether the plant can actually make setpoint.
  • Narrow deadband only when operations will accept the extra movement that comes with it.

Likely causes

  • proportionalConstant is low for the throttling range you need.
  • integralConstant is very small, so the offset trims over many minutes.
  • rampTime is long, and the output is still slewing after the error has closed.
  • executeTime is slow on a loop that should be quick.

LoopPoint adjustments

  • Raise proportionalConstant about 20% at a time until a setpoint step produces a clear move. Stop before it rings.
  • Then bring integralConstant up from the low end of the starting range.
  • Shorten rampTime if the output is still crawling with the error already gone.
  • Leave a zone slower than a static-pressure loop. The space is the slow element.

Likely causes

  • The loop stayed enabled while the fan or pump was off, and the integral wound up.
  • rampTime is 0.
  • disableAction forces Max when the loop drops out.
  • bias is high and the proportional term is large at the moment of enable.

LoopPoint adjustments

  • Link loopEnable to the fan or pump status.
  • Set rampTime to a stroke you can accept: 20–60 s on a valve, 10–40 s on a supply fan.
  • Choose a disableAction that matches the fail position. Zero or Min is typical for a cooling valve.
  • After enable, out should slew. A step to 0 or 100 means the ramp is not in the path.

Likely causes

  • loopAction is reversed for this plant.
  • A cooling valve and a heating valve had their actions swapped.
  • The actuator signal itself is reversed at the AnalogOutput or at the device.

LoopPoint adjustments

  • Cooling coil and chilled water: Direct. Output rises when temperature rises.
  • Heating coil, duct static, and VAV airflow: Reverse. Output falls when the variable rises.
  • Flip loopAction and repeat the setpoint step before you change a gain.
  • If the action is right and the device still travels the wrong way, correct the actuator polarity. Gains will not undo a reversed end device.

05 — Commissioning

Six steps on a live air handler

Do them in this order. A gain change on a backwards valve or a stuck damper only hides the fault.

  1. Prove the device, the sensor, and the stroke

    The controlled variable has to be a live number. Drive the output 0 to 100 and watch the valve, damper, or VFD follow. A closed hand valve, a failed transducer, or a fan that is off will not tune.

  2. Verify loopAction

    Direct when more output drives the variable down: cooling and chilled water. Reverse when more output drives it up: heating, duct static, VAV airflow. Enable the loop and watch one small move. If it runs to the stop, flip the action and leave the gains alone until it moves the right way.

  3. Start proportional only

    Set integralConstant to 0 and derivativeConstant to 0. Leave bias at 0 unless you already know the output that holds this setpoint. You should see a stable response and a standing offset. That offset is the reason the next step exists.

  4. Dial in proportionalConstant

    Start from output span divided by the throttling range. Raise it until a setpoint step produces a definite move in the controlled variable. If the trend rings and the rings do not shrink, back it off.

  5. Add integralConstant gently

    Start at the low end of the range for that loop. Remember the units: more repeats per minute removes offset faster and overshoots more. Wait until the reset has finished its work before you add any more.

  6. Set rampTime and deadband, then trend a step

    Put rampTime near the actuator stroke and deadband at the noise floor. History the setpoint, the controlled variable, and out on one chart. Step the setpoint. Leave the loop when that trend settles without a slam, a hunt, or an offset you cannot accept.

Starting values are where a trend begins. The plant in front of you decides the final slots. LoopPoint is the PID in the Niagara kitControl palette.
